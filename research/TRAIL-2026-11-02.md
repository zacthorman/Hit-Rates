# The paper trail: why a player line landed, and which reasons predict the next one

Written 2 November 2026. Code in `research/trail/`: `extract.py` (on the Mac, reads `cache/` only), `build.py`, `test_drivers.py`, `trail.py`. Results in `drivers.csv` and `trail_effects.csv`.

## The question

A player goes 0, 0, 0, 0 and then 1. What happened in the game it landed? If the answer repeats (his direct opponent fouls a lot, his team was behind, his opponent dribbles at him), it should show up before the next game too, and that is where an edge would be. The rule from this work: **the game state may only claim a reason that predicted results on games it was not fitted on.**

## Data

From the SofaScore cache, 7,765 football matches with line-ups, 230,182 player-games:

| Source | Matches | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| Line-ups | 7,765 | each player's minutes, fouls, fouled, tackles, shots, dribbles, duels, touches, interceptions... |
| Average positions | about 3,000 | where every player actually stood |
| Incidents | about 3,200 | goals, cards and substitutions with the minute, so the score at any minute |
| Shot maps | about 3,100 | every shot: minute, situation (open play, corner, set piece, counter), xG, where from |
| Momentum graph | about 3,100 | who had the territory, minute by minute |
| Team statistics | about 9,300 | possession, fouls, shots and the rest |

**Direct opponents** are paired one to one from the two teams' outfield starters. The pairing uses each player's average position over his previous 10 games, mirrored, with an optimal assignment. So it is known before kick-off. 35,246 starter-games have a direct opponent and enough history (5+ previous games).

**Not in the data:** who fouled whom. Only per-player totals exist, so a foul is traced to the man he was paired against, not to the actual foul.

## 1. Before kick-off: does the direct opponent predict anything?

Poisson models with minutes as exposure. Base: his own rate over his last 10 games, the opposing team's usual figure, and home or away. Fitted on the first 70% of games by date, scored on the last 30% (10,510 player-games). The gain is the improvement in out-of-sample log-likelihood per 1,000 player-games. The effect is the elasticity: a 1.0 coefficient means doubling the driver doubles the count.

| His stat | Added driver (his direct opponent's usual rate) | Effect | p | Out-of-sample gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tackles** | **opponent's dribbles per 90** | **+0.072** | <0.001 | **+4.6** |
| **Tackles** | **opponent's possession losses per 90** | **+0.139** | <0.001 | **+2.8** |
| Shots | opponent's interceptions per 90 | +0.062 | <0.001 | +2.1 |
| Fouled | opponent's tackles per 90 | +0.053 | <0.001 | +0.9 |
| Fouls | opponent's fouls won per 90 | +0.026 | 0.002 | +0.1 |
| Fouled | **opponent's fouls per 90** | +0.022 | 0.05 | **-0.1 (worse)** |
| Fouls | opponent's dribbles per 90 | -0.011 | 0.06 | +0.1 |

**Tackles are the one clear win.** A man marking a dribbler makes more tackles, and you know who he is marking before the game. "His opponent is a fouler, so he will be fouled" does **not** predict: it made the out-of-sample fit worse. The other effects are small.

## 2. After the fact: what was different in the game?

Same Poisson set-up with in-game context. Rate ratios (x1.10 = 10% more). n = 34,968.

| His stat | Context | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Fouled | +1 foul committed by his direct opponent that game | **x1.175** |
| Fouled | +10 points of team possession | x1.03 |
| Fouls | +1 foul won by his direct opponent that game | **x1.160** |
| Fouls | +10 points of team possession | x0.966 |
| Tackles | +1 dribble by his direct opponent that game | **x1.069** |
| Tackles | +10 points of team possession | x0.953 |
| Tackles | ahead the whole time (against level) | x1.051 |
| Shots | +10 points of momentum share | **x1.172** |
| Shots | +10 points of possession | **x0.895** |
| Shots | behind or ahead the whole time (against level) | x1.12 each |

The trail is real in the game itself: when his direct opponent fouled more, he was fouled more, and the reverse for fouls committed. Shots follow territory (momentum), not possession: a team that pins the other back shoots more, a team that only keeps the ball does not.

## 3. Streak breaks: 1+ landed after 3+ straight misses

Means, against the same players' other misses.

| Stat | Games | Opponent did (that game) | Opponent usually (per 90) | Possession | Momentum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fouled, landed | 1,760 | 1.12 fouls | 1.15 | 52.1 | 0.54 |
| Fouled, missed | 2,504 | 0.85 fouls | 1.16 | 51.3 | 0.53 |
| Tackles, landed | 1,003 | 1.01 dribbles | **1.13** | **50.8** | 0.52 |
| Tackles, missed | 1,502 | 0.58 dribbles | **0.78** | **54.0** | 0.58 |
| Shots, landed | 1,601 | 1.24 tackles | 1.41 | **52.5** | **0.56** |
| Shots, missed | 2,694 | 1.19 tackles | 1.36 | **49.8** | **0.48** |

- **Fouled:** the break came when his opponent happened to foul more (1.12 against 0.85), but that opponent was no more of a fouler usually (1.15 against 1.16). It is visible after the fact and not predictable before it.
- **Tackles:** the break came against opponents who usually dribble more (1.13 against 0.78) and when his team had less of the ball. Both can be known before kick-off.
- **Shots:** the break came with more territory and a little more of the ball.

## What it means for the game state

1. **Claim only what passed.** The page may say "faces a dribbler, expect tackles" and "the side with the territory shoots". It should not say "faces a fouler, expect to be fouled" or "the pivot is outnumbered, expect fouls" (FINDINGS.md tested that too and found nothing). Each reason shown should carry the number behind it.
2. **Put the tackles driver into the model.** The direct opponent's dribble rate and the expected possession, both known before kick-off, then judged on the Bet365 log (Bet365 prices tackles; Paddy Power does not).
3. **Put expected territory, not possession, behind shots.**
4. **Show odds from every book** beside the reason (Paddy Power, Bet365, Oddschecker's median, Pinnacle where it prices the line), so the explanation and the price sit together.

## What more data would add

The biggest gap is **who fouled whom, and where**. With that, fouls could be traced to the actual man, not the nearest one. Candidates to check:

- **Live commentary feeds** that write "Foul by X. Y wins a free kick in the defensive half" (ESPN and others publish Opta-style commentary). This gives the fouler, the man fouled and the zone, for every foul.
- **SofaScore's per-player heatmaps** for each match. More precise than average positions for flank and pressing roles.
- **Pressing and pass-network data** (PPDA by zone, who receives between the lines). Usually on Opta-based sites, which often block automated access, so check each before building on it.
