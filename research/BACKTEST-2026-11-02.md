# Backtest / audit of the pipeline's logged Paddy Power prices

## Executive summary

1. **Nothing is proven profitable.** No broad market type makes money at the first logged price, in any sport. The baselines lose:
   NBA main overs -11.6%, main unders -2.9%, alt overs -27.8%; NFL main overs -11.4%, unders -6.2%, alt overs -14.1%; soccer player props -50.7%; soccer team markets -10% to -44% by stat.
2. **Structural pattern (robust, but not profitable):** in NBA and NFL main lines the **under is far cheaper than the over**. NBA unders lose about 3% (near fair) against 12% for overs, in both halves.
   Paddy shades the over. If you must bet NBA/NFL main lines, the under side is where any edge from a model would have to start.
3. **Best candidate, unproven:** **NFL QB rushing-yards main unders**: n=53, 27 games, hit 67.9% at ~1.83, ROI +24.2% (90% CI +3.8% to +43.3%), H1 +14.7%, H2 +34.1%. The overs were -41%.
   Mechanism: QB rushing is right-skewed (kneel-downs, scrambles), so the median falls below a mean-based line (median value minus line = -2.5 yds). It is one of ~1,000 slices and covers 2.5 weeks, so treat it as a hypothesis to track live, not a proven edge.
4. **Rules that looked good and then failed robustness:** NBA "drift after a miss" (+15.8% in one dedupe) is negative or noise in 27 of 27 pre-specified variants and loses -14.6% without its top 3 players.
   The 10/10 alt-over "banker" rule hits close to its implied rate and is flat to negative (NBA -2.2%, NFL -10.8%, soccer -15.1%).
5. **Clear losers to drop:** all soccer player props (every market, every price band, starters included), alt overs priced 3.0+ in every sport, NBA main overs, NBA/NFL "9/10" and "5/5" short alt legs as bankers,
   team shots / shots-on-target overs, team card overs outside big games, and any bet chosen because the model's edge is large. Positive-model-edge buckets lose in NBA and soccer.
6. **The model:** in NBA/NFL the Brier score is worse than the bookmaker's implied probability, and the model is overconfident above 60%. In soccer the model is itself overconfident in the 0.3-0.5 range (0.35 predicted, 0.23 actual; this includes substitute appearances), while Paddy's implied probabilities sit even higher,
   so the model's "edges" there are illusory, and positive-edge soccer bets lose 30-70%.
7. **CLV:** prices rarely move between first and last log (6-20% of rows), and the movement does not predict results in any usable way. Betting at the last price changes ROI by under 1 point overall.

## Data and cleaning

* Player log: 112,455 rows. Settled win/lose 88,244; void 14,759; unsettled 9,452 (excluded).
  Types coerced (prices, chances, lines, k/n counts, minutes to float; `over`/`started` to bool).
* Main line = both sides logged for the same event/player/stat/line; alt = one side only. Soccer player props are all one-sided (alt overs).
  Main lines were deduped to **one row per player-stat-game-side** (the line first logged; later re-lined rows dropped), e.g. Lamar Jackson rushing 25.5 and 26.5 count once.
* **No DNP voiding applied.** Following the coordinator's correction, all-zero rows are graded as the book graded them (e.g. Lamar Jackson played one snap). They are kept in every main
  number and reported separately as **early-exit risk**:
  NBA 13 player-games (410 rows, 1.4%) with minutes < 50% of season average. Their overs hit ~3-6%, unders ~98%, and they account for 2.1% of all losing NBA over rows.
  NFL 18 player-games (320 rows) where every yards/receptions stat settled 0 despite prior usage (Lamar Jackson, Zay Flowers [5 targets, 0 catches], Jake Ferguson, Evan Engram, Cade Otton...). All overs lost and all unders won.

* Picks: 7,180 records (no duplicates found across snapshots).

**Multiple testing.** About 1,024 slices were evaluated. At a one-sided 5% tail you would expect around 51 slices to look "significantly positive" by chance alone.
The positive slices found here are of that order. Only rules that are simple, pre-specified, mechanistically plausible and stable over time deserve any weight, and even those need fresh out-of-sample data.

### Conclusions (Q1, Q5, CLV)
* Every sport x market type loses at the first price. NBA/NFL main unders are the least bad (-3% / -6%, CI includes 0). Main overs lose about 11-12%.
* Alt overs get worse the longer the price: at 3.0+ they lose 40% (NBA), 18% (NFL) and 58% (soccer). Short alt rungs (<1.25) lose 8-9% in NBA/NFL.
* By stat, NBA main unders on Assists, Points+Assists and PRA are break-even (about 0%). Nothing is positive with a CI excluding 0.
* **Calibration:** NBA/NFL model probabilities are roughly calibrated below 0.6 and overconfident above (0.65 predicted becomes 0.60 actual; 0.85 becomes 0.77-0.81). The book's implied probability has a better Brier score than the model in NBA and NFL.
  Soccer: the model overpredicts in the 0.2-0.5 range (by 10-12 points) but sits well under Paddy's implied probability. For 80+ minute starters it matches outcomes closely. ROI by model edge is negative in almost every bucket. **Model edge is not a usable bet filter.**
* **CLV:** only 9% (NBA), 6% (NFL) and 20% (soccer) of rows moved. NBA bets that drifted lost more at the first price (expected), but steamed bets did not win at the last price. CLV is too sparse here to validate the model.

## Caveats
* Two weeks of data (NBA 88 games, NFL 27, soccer 224 player-prop games / 213 team-market games). Every interval is wide, and the NFL is close to anecdotal.
* "First price" is the first time the pipeline logged the line (median 8-16 h before kick-off), not necessarily when a real bettor could have got on at stake.
* Settlement comes from the pipeline's own feed, not Paddy's settlement. Soccer shot/foul counts may differ from Paddy's (see section 8).
* Early-exit / all-zero rows are kept (per the coordinator's correction), which hurts overs by about 2% of losses in NBA.
* About 1,000 slices were tested. Expect dozens of false positives; only the robustness-checked items above deserve follow-up.
* Scripts: `/home/claude/bt/scripts/` (common.py, boxdb.py, s1_baseline.py ... s7_robust.py, assemble.py). Re-run in order: boxdb.py, s1 to s7, assemble.py (intermediate pickles go to /tmp/claude-0).
