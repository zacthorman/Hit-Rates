## 5. The La Liga divide by zero

`USAGE.md` (14 October) recorded that "La Liga crashed the replay in `hitrates.league_ratings` (a divide by zero, pre-existing, not chased)".

**Cause.** Every division in `league_ratings` is guarded, so a plain zero cannot reach one. The guard on the iterative fit was:

```python
if dfn[opp] > 0:
    a_vals.append(mine / (base * dfn[opp]))
if att[opp] > 0:
    d_vals.append(theirs / (base * att[opp]))
```

It tests the rating, not the product. On a thin early-season fit the 25 passes do not converge: they collapse. One side's rating hits exactly 0, and its opponents' ratings then shrink faster than geometrically. Tracing La Liga's first-half offsides on this cache, the smallest defence rating went 1e-16, 1e-25, 1e-40, 1e-63, 1e-100, 1e-161, 1e-259 over passes 14 to 20. Once a rating is subnormal (below about 1e-308), multiplying it by a small league average rounds to exactly 0.0, while the rating itself still tests above zero. First-half red cards, with an average of about 0.23, are the obvious candidate. The division then raises.

**Failing case.** On this cache La Liga's fits collapse (to 3e-257) but stop short of the underflow on every one of its 333 fit days, so the 13 October crash itself depended on the Mac's data at the time. The identical failure does reproduce on cached data in the Premier League. The fit as of Wolverhampton v Newcastle United (18 Jan 2026) raises on HEAD:

```
  File "hitrates.py", line 1911, in league_ratings
    d_vals.append(theirs / (base * att[opp]))
ZeroDivisionError: float division by zero
```

The stat is first-half red cards: 43 of 426 records carry a value, and the league average is 0.23. That fit's records, cut down to the one stat, are now `tests/fixtures/league_ratings_pl_red_cards_1st_2026-01-18.json`.

**Fix** (`hitrates.py`): guard the product.

```python
den = base * dfn[opp]
if den > 0:
    a_vals.append(mine / den)
den = base * att[opp]
if den > 0:
    d_vals.append(theirs / den)
```

A collapsed rating carries no information, so the observation is skipped, exactly as it already was for a zero rating. Wherever the product is positive the arithmetic is the same, so every fit that did not crash is unchanged.

**Proof.**

|---|---|
| HEAD `hitrates.py` | 2 failed (`ZeroDivisionError`, hitrates.py:1911), 1 passed |
| Fixed | 3 passed |

The tests are the real failing fit (it must not raise, and every rating must be finite and non-negative), plus a synthetic four-side league with known multipliers that the fit must recover, so the fix cannot have broken an ordinary fit.

## Side effect worth knowing

The first version of this note blamed importing `weekend.py`. That was too broad: a bare import writes nothing. ### a. Every lead can settle

**Cause.** `tonight.py` picks each fixture's lead on the game-state chance (`tonight_chance`). `besttrack.snapshot` records each fixture's top three singles ranked on `weekend.sort_value`. Nothing made the lead one of those three, and `tonight.results_block` can only settle a lead whose exact leg is in `best_bets.json`. Of the 53 leads logged on 21 and 22 October, 15 had no leg there.

**Fix.** `besttrack.track_leads` adds a "Lead" item for every logged lead whose exact leg (event, team, player, stat, period, line, side) is not already in the record. It is not a back-dated pick: `lead_log.json` only takes a lead before kick-off, the item's `snapshot_at` is when the lead was written, and a lead written at or after kick-off is refused. A lead that already has a leg gets no copy, so calibration does not count it twice. `snapshot` runs it, and so does `settle`, so a lead gets a leg to settle even on a run where no snapshot was taken. `tonight.write_leads` now stores the sport, which `settle` needs for player legs; older rows take it from the record or the competition.

### b. Darts leads

The question was whether to give darts a proper chance or drop it from the leads. The evidence is every darts leg the site has published and settled (`best_bets.json`, distinct legs):

| Chance | Said | Landed | Brier | Log loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All settled darts legs (190) | | 66.3% | | |
| raw record | 62.9% | 66.3% | 0.2254 | 0.6402 |
| shrunk (h+2)/(n+4) | 59.8% | 66.3% | 0.2258 | 0.6425 |
| 1 / fair | 62.5% | 66.3% | 0.2260 | 0.6422 |
| flat (hit rate) | 66.3% | 66.3% | 0.2234 | 0.6389 |
| **Best leg per match, as a lead is picked (52)** | | 71.2% | | |
| raw record | 70.1% | 71.2% | 0.2029 | 0.5863 |
| shrunk (h+2)/(n+4) | 65.3% | 71.2% | 0.2049 | 0.5964 |
| 1 / fair | 69.4% | 71.2% | 0.2047 | 0.5926 |
| flat (hit rate) | 71.2% | 71.2% | 0.2053 | 0.6008 |

Two things follow. The chances are honest or cautious in level: on lead-like picks no candidate overstates. But they barely rank: across all 190 legs none beats a flat rate, and on the 52 lead-like legs the gaps between candidates are noise. That is the case for keeping darts with a chance, not for dropping it. Exclusion would throw away legs that land 71% for a defect in the label, and leaving 0 hides them from calibration.

**Fix.** `tonight_chance` (now a module-level function, so it can be tested) gives darts legs `weekend.chance`, the shrunk record the weekend picks already use for darts. It is the most cautious candidate (65% said, 71% landed on lead-like picks), so a darts lead's stated chance should come in under what lands rather than over. Every other sport is unchanged: a leg the game state cannot reach still ranks on its own calibrated chance or last. Darts leads are now ranked on chance times the square root of the price, like every other lead, instead of on sample size alone. Watch the darts rows of the results card: if darts leads keep landing well above their stated chance after 100 or so, the raw record is the better chance.

A wider fault turned up here, not fixed because it changes every sport's picks: legs from `weekend.legs_for` carry no sport (`make_index.read_entries` puts none in `meta`), so `weekend._is_football` reads every leg as football and `weekend.sort_value` ranks darts, NBA and NFL legs on the football chance.

### c. League names

`tournament_id_for` already folded case and punctuation, but two scripts still bypassed it, and nothing else used the same rule:

`sofascore_api.competition_key` is the one spelling: lower case, accents and punctuation gone, and anything after the first comma dropped. "Serie A" still means Italy; the Brazilian aliases are full names only. A bare number still works as an id.

### d. learned.json

`learner.model()` wrote `learned.json` on its first call in any process, so pricing a single leg (`tests/test_strength.py`, the strength replay, any script) rewrote a committed file with a fit from whatever `best_bets.json` it saw. Nothing reads the file back; it is the record of the fit the picks card reports. Now `model()` only fits, and `learner.save()` writes. `weekend.picks()`, which builds that card in the publishing run, calls `save()`, as does `python learner.py`. The publishing run still writes the file exactly as before; the tests, replays and one-off scripts no longer do.
